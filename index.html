CASE 1 · THE HERO

The Visitor From Between the Stars

Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS crossed the SPHEREx sky in August 2025 and again in December 2025. Follow the case: find the visitor, check it against the star charts, and measure how far it travelled.

Case data: being prepared from the IRSA 3I/ATLAS observation table.

CASE 2

The Two-Epoch Check

Our named method for vetting a candidate mover: it must appear in both epochs, shifted consistently; it must not be a known Gaia star sitting at the new position; and it must survive the difference view. Anything less is a cosmic ray or a ghost.

CASE 3

The Star That Barely Moves

Barnard's Star has the largest proper motion of any star — and at SPHEREx's 6.2-arcsecond pixels even it shifts by only a pixel or two per year. Case 3 is the honest measurement case: centroid the star in each epoch and compare with Gaia's prediction, error bars included.

How it works

  1. Query. The archive (IRSA) is searched for every SPHEREx observation of your patch of sky, in the current data releases.
  2. Align. Cutouts are reprojected onto one shared north-up grid, with one shared brightness stretch across all epochs.
  3. Blink. Frames flip in place. Stars stay still; movers jump, variables pulse, and artefacts fail the Two-Epoch Check.

About & data

SkyBlink was built by Team Sohan Halwa (Daim Ali & Ali Abdullah) for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge “Planet X and SPHEREx”.

Data & services: SPHEREx Level-2 images and the IRSA archive (NASA/JPL-Caltech & the SPHEREx team, via IRSA at IPAC) · ESA Gaia (DPAC) for the reference star positions and proper motions · WISE (NASA/JPL-Caltech) · SIMBAD (CDS) for name resolution · SkyBoT (IMCCE) for solar-system object positions · Aladin Lite (CDS) technology for sky browsing.
