What to look for

—

Frame info

Each date is the exposure's own observation date, read from its FITS DATE-OBS header (never the file processing date). All frames of a target share one grid and one brightness stretch, so blinking shows the sky changing — not the scaling. Each SPHEREx detector sees a different wavelength band, though, so a frame can also change brightness because of colour; every frame's band is labelled, and “Same band only” compares like with like.

Data freshness

SPHEREx DR1 (surveys 1–2, reprocessed) + QR3 (surveys 3–4)

CASE 1 · THE HERO

The Visitor From Between the Stars

Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS crossed the SPHEREx sky in August 2025 and again in December 2025. Follow the case: find the visitor, check it against the star charts, and measure how far it travelled.

Case data: live — real SPHEREx frames at the comet's per-visit positions (IRSA 3I/ATLAS table).

CASE 2

The Two-Epoch Check

Our named method for vetting a candidate mover: it must appear in both epochs, shifted consistently; it must not be a known Gaia star sitting at the new position; and it must survive the difference view. Anything less is a cosmic ray or a ghost.

CASE 3

The Star That Barely Moves

Barnard's Star has the largest proper motion of any star — and at SPHEREx's native 6.15-arcsecond pixels even it shifts by only ~1.7 pixels per year (~3.4 of our 3.1″ rendered pixels). Case 3 is the honest measurement case: centroid the star in each epoch and compare with Gaia's prediction, error bars included.

How it works

  1. Query. The archive (IRSA) is searched for every SPHEREx observation of your patch of sky, in the current data releases.
  2. Align. Cutouts are reprojected onto one shared north-up grid, with one shared brightness stretch across all epochs.
  3. Blink. Frames flip in place. Stars stay still; movers jump, variables pulse, and artefacts are screened out by the Two-Epoch Check — our vetting method, applied in the validation on the Methods page.
